Plasmodium is a genus of unicellular eukaryotes that are obligate parasites of vertebrates and insects such as mosquitoes in the genus Anopheles. The life cycles of Plasmodium species involve development in a blood-feeding insect host which then injects parasites into a vertebrate host during a blood meal. Over 200 species of Plasmodium have been described, many of which have been subdivided into 14 subgenera based on parasite morphology and host range. P. falciparum is therefore regarded as the deadliest parasite in humans. Plasmodium malariae is a parasitic protozoan that causes malaria in humans. While the natural warm-blooded hosts of P. knowlesi are likely various Old World monkeys, humans can be infected by P. knowlesi if they are fed upon by infected mosquitoes. It is most closely related to the human parasite Plasmodium vivax as well as other Plasmodium species that infect non-human primates. Diagnosis of P. knowlesi infection is challenging as P. knowlesi very closely resembles other species that infect humans.
