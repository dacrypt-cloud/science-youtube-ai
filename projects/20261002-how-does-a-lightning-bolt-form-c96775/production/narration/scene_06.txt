Lightning involves a near-instantaneous release of energy on a scale averaging between 200 megajoules and 7 gigajoules. A more specific hypothesis, which has been supported by past observational and laboratory charging studies, suggests that broad, strong updrafts and associated large liquid water contents in severe storms lead to the generation of an inverted charge structure Physical mechanism of initial breakdown pulses and narrow bipolar events in lightning discharges. We have used machine learning techniques to successfully hindcast nearby and distant lightning hazards by looking at single-site observations of meteorological parameters. Most NBEs are quite peculiar in the sense that very frequently they occur in isolation from other lightning processes. The results encourage further analysis on how mining techniques could contribute to further our understanding of lightning dependencies on atmospheric parameters.
